Two below Transfers

1NT - 4♣/4♦ is a transfer to ♥/♠ with slam interest. Opener breaks the transfer to encourage. 4♠/4NT is then RKCB depending on the strain.

1NT - 4♥/4♠ are pre-emptive to play, but prefer a transfer at the 2 level followed by a raise to 4 (shows no slam interest). A splinter after a 2-level transfer shows mild slam interest.

Modified Inverted Minors

Inverted minors flip the traditional meaning of single and double minor-suit raises. Playing inverted minors, a single raise of a minor opening is strong and a jump raise is weak.

The Strong Raise

Auctions

The single raise (i.e. 1♣:2♣ and 1♦:2♦) is forcing for one round. The requirements are:

  • 10+ points
  • At least 4-card support
  • No 4-card major

Responder will often be unbalanced, since he can make notrump responses with balanced hands.

With 4-3-3-3 shape, 1NT is more descriptive than an inverted raise.

The Weak Raise

Conversely, the requirements for a weak jump raise (i.e. 1♣:3♣ or 1♦:3♦) are:

  • 6-8 points
  • At least 5-card support
  • No 4-card major

2♠ Natural, 3+ cards. Forcing.
2NT 18-19 balanced

Rebid Meaning

2♠/♠ Natural, 3+ cards. Forcing.

3♣ Natural 5+ card suit, not forcing.

In Competition

In competition, inverted minors are off for most partnerships. A single raise is weak with 4-card support, and a jump raise is weak with 5+ card support.

If the opponents have doubled for takeout over 1♣/♦, then responder can use the Jordan 2NT convention to show a limit raise or better.

If the opponents have made a natural suit overcall over 1♣/♦, then responder can cuebid to show a limit raise or better.

Other Considerations

Because inverted minors use 0-6 points for weak raises and 10+ points for strong ones, responder will sometimes be stuck for a bid with 7-9 points. These hands, which would traditionally make a single raise, can no longer do so. The typical approach is to respond 1NT even with unbalanced shape.

Two-Way Checkback

2♣ = invite or stop in diamonds; 2♦ = game force.

Two-way checkback separates responder’s strength while leaving room to find a major-suit fit after opener rebids 1NT.

Responder’s choices

The following treatment uses direct 2NT as a club relay. This is an agreement to confirm, not a universal part of checkback.

Rebid over 1NT Meaning
2♣ Artificial; opener must bid 2♦. Responder passes or makes a natural invitation
2♦ Artificial game force; need not hold diamonds, even if opener bid them

2♥ after responding 1♠ Weak, 5+ spades and 4+ hearts; opener passes or gives preference

| 3x | Weak, 6 card suit (raise or shift)

2NT Trump Ask

After opener raises responder’s major to two, responder’s 2NT asks about opener’s support and strength.

Responder has invitational values or better. The main question is whether opener has three or four trumps: with only four cards in responder’s major, an eight-card fit is not yet certain.

2NT is artificial and forcing for one round, not automatically game-forcing. Opener cannot pass it. A weak responder normally passes the original raise; using the enquiry commits the partnership to at least the three level.

Opener’s replies

Use the same four steps over either major:

Reply to 2NT Support for responder’s major Strength
3♣ Three cards Minimum
3♦ Three cards Maximum
3♥ Four cards Minimum
3♠ Four cards Maximum

Remember 3, 3, 4, 4, with minimum before maximum. These are artificial replies: after a heart raise, 3♠ shows four hearts, not spades.

Responder’s follow-ups

The following is a simple working agreement for these notes:

Information received Practical continuation
Minimum, three-card support With an invitation, normally stop in three of the major; consider an agreed minor-suit escape if the 4–3 fit looks poor
Maximum, three-card support With an invitation, normally bid game; consider 3NT with only four trumps and adequate side-suit protection
Minimum, four-card support An invitation can stop in three of the major; a game-going hand bids game
Maximum, four-card support Normally bid four of the major; consider 3NT with suitable balanced hands

A return to three of the agreed major is a sign-off where available: opener has already described their strength. After a heart raise, the maximum four-card reply of 3♠ passes 3♥, so the partnership is committed to game.

For further exploration, use a new suit as forcing: a stopper-showing bid while investigating notrump after a three-card reply, or a control-bid once a fit and slam interest are established.

3NT is a natural game suggestion, not another artificial enquiry. With a known 4–3 fit it will often be preferable to four of the major, but the trump count alone does not establish side-suit stoppers.

Good-Bad 2NT

Via 2NT = competitive; direct = extras.

In agreed competitive auctions, artificial 2NT separates hands that merely want to compete from stronger hands bidding a suit directly. It commonly applies to opener’s rebid after right-hand opponent bids a suit at the two level.

Basic mechanism

Action Meaning
2NT, then a suit Weaker competitive action
Direct suit bid at the three level Stronger action

When the opponents pass, partner normally continues with 3♣. The 2NT bidder can pass with clubs or correct to the intended suit. This is a Lebensohl-style mechanism; it does not import every agreement from .

“Good” is relative to the auction: a direct bid may be invitational, or stronger if the partnership has already invited game. Avoid assigning one fixed point range to every sequence.

When it is off

The BridgeHands treatment excludes auctions where:

  • RHO passes, doubles, bids at the one level, or makes a cuebid or conventional notrump call.
  • RHO makes a natural two-level overcall showing opening values, rather than the competitive action required by the agreement.
  • The opponents begin with a strong artificial club.
  • Partner opened 1NT: use the agreed defence, including where applicable.

Partnership points to confirm

After 1♦ - (Pass) - 1♥ - (2♠), some pairs use Good-Bad 2NT; others retain natural 2NT with a stopper. Agree this explicitly.

Also settle when partner may depart from 3♣, how to show strong balanced hands, and what happens if opponents bid over 2NT. The weak route can lose the chance to show its suit if the opponents raise again.

Unusual over Unusual

Over a 2 suited overcall, a cue bid of the higher or known suit is a good raise of partner’s suit. Any raise of partners suit is a weak raise.

With a known second suit, a cue bid of the lower suit shows a competitive raise in the fourth suit, and a bid of that suit is weaker.

A double is penalty-oriented: subsequent doubles by either partner are penalties. Do not double with general values, re-open with a x to compete.

Note that many expert players play the lower cue as the good raise. This is prone to being forgotten, keep the good raise the same for one suit known and two suit known bids.

Smolen

Bid the four-card major to show five in the other major.

Smolen is used after a 1NT opening when responder has 5–4 in the majors and game-forcing values. It helps find a 5–3 fit while keeping the notrump opener as declarer.

Responder’s bids

Start with 2♣ Stayman. If opener shows a four-card major, raise to game with ordinary game-going strength.

If opener bids 2♦, denying a four-card major:

Responder’s jump Meaning
3♥ Four hearts and five spades; game-forcing
3♠ Four spades and five hearts; game-forcing

Opener’s continuation

  • With three cards in responder’s five-card major, show support and reach game in that suit.
  • With only two cards in that major, bid 3NT.

Neither Smolen bid can be passed. Responder with slam interest can continue exploring after a fit is found.

Namyats

Namyats is a convention for distinguishing weak and strong 4♥/♠ opening bids. It uses 4♣ and 4♦ opening bids to show strong hands with hearts and spades, respectively. This can help responder gauge his hand when considering whether to bid. The philosophy is that improving your slam-sniffing toolset is more important than 4-level minor suit preempts (the latter often inviting the opponents to bid 4-of-a-major anyway).

Opener’s Bids

Opening Meaning
4♣ 7+ strong hearts and 8-9 tricks. Example: ♠A J♥K Q J 10 9 4 3 2♦K Q♣3
4♦ 7+ strong spades and 8-9 tricks. Example: ♠A K Q 10 8 7 6♥K 5♦A 2♣6 3
4♥ 7+ hearts and 7-8 tricks. Weaker than 4. Example: ♠9 8♥A Q 10 8 7 5 3 2♦A♣J 3
4♠ 7+ spades and 7-8 tricks. Weaker than 4. Example: ♠A K J 6 5 4 3 2♥Q 3 2♦J♣10

Note that other opening bids are also possible with similar one-suited hands:

Opening Response
1♥/♠ Natural. Used when the hand is too weak to open at the 4-level (e.g. only a 5-card major suit).
2♣ Playing Standard American methods, this is strong and artificial. Used when the hand is too strong to use Namyats.
2♥/♠ Playing Standard American methods, these bids are weak two-bids - preemptive.
3♥/♠ Playing Standard American methods, these bids are also preemptive.

Responder’s Bids

In response to 4♣

Opening Response
4♦ Artificial relay, showing slam interest in hearts. Opener is expected to clarify his hand.
4♥ Signoff, showing no interest in slam. Opener is expected to pass.
4♠ Natural, showing a big one-suited hand in spades. Very rare. Not forcing.

In response to 4♦

Bid Meaning
4♥ Artificial relay, showing slam interest in spades. Opener is expected to clarify his hand.
4♠ Signoff, showing no interest in slam. Opener is expected to pass.

In response to 4♥/♠

Responder may make a conventional reply like 4NT Blackwood, 5NT Grand Slam Force, etc. These responses are based on partnership agreement.

Opener’s Rebids

There are many flavors of Namyats used after a 4♦/♥ relay. The simplest approach is:

  • After a 4♦/♥ relay from responder, a 4♥/♠ bid by opener is a natural signoff.
  • All other bids are natural or follow partnership agreements (e.g. cue bids, 4NT Blackwood, etc.).

In Competition

Interference

As stated above, this convention is never on if they interfere after our 1MAJ opening. However, we do need to cope if they interfere after the 2NT response:

Bid Meaning
Opener’s double Shortness in suit doubled
Opener’s new suits natural or a control
3NT Balanced Maximum (Ace or King in their suit)
Opener’s Pass Nothing special–flat hand, could be 5x3x2 awful after which, responder’s X=penalty
Opener’s jump to 4M dead minimum, but 6x3x2

If they double Asks or re-asks : XX = business, Pass=S1, etc. If they bid after Ask or re-ask : X=Penalty, Pass=S1, etc. (except when double = short as above)

Summary

In summary then, our new responses:

  • Allow opener to limit his hand
  • Allow opener to describe his shape
  • When responder holds a singleton, allow responder to describe his shape and limit his hand
  • Allow responder to bid game without revealing opener’s shape when both hands are minimum

Pudding raise

Over 1M, 3NT is a “pudding raise” showing 3 of the suit and a balanced hand with a maximum of 15 points. It offers opener the chance to choose the best game or continue with a cue bid. 4NT is RCKB

Rebids after 2-over-1 response to a major

When responding to a two-over-one response to a major-suit opening, rebidding the original response suit normally shows six or more cards, and the auction remains game-forcing, but there are exceptions. A new suit is bid when it is the most descriptive call available, which may be 3 cards in ♦ but never in a major. A notrump rebid suggests a reasonably balanced hand, interest in playing in notrump, and a particular strength range.

After opener rebids at the two level, a jump to 3NT shows about 15–17 HCP. A rebid of 2NT shows either a good 12 to a poor 15 HCP or 18+ HCP. With the lower range, responder has a minimum for the two-over-one response and usually shows stoppers in any unbid suits. With 18+ HCP, responder also rebids 2NT, then bids 4NT if opener raises to 3NT, showing the extra strength.

Repeating a 5 card suit

♠K J 10 9 4♥A K 6♦9 6 3♣Q 8

There are some hands where opener should repeat a 5 card suit. Over 2♦/2♣ responder should bid 2♠. Responder should expect that this rebid bid of a 5 card suit is possible.
