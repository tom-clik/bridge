**Via 2NT = competitive; direct = extras.**

In agreed competitive auctions, artificial 2NT separates hands that merely want to compete from stronger hands bidding a suit directly. It commonly applies to opener's rebid after right-hand opponent bids a suit at the two level.

## Basic mechanism

| Action                             | Meaning                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 2NT, then a suit                   | Weaker competitive action |
| Direct suit bid at the three level | Stronger action           |
{.styled}

When the opponents pass, partner normally continues with 3♣. The 2NT bidder can pass with clubs or correct to the intended suit. This is a Lebensohl-style mechanism; it does not import every agreement from [](#Lebensohl).

“Good” is relative to the auction: a direct bid may be invitational, or stronger if the partnership has already invited game. Avoid assigning one fixed point range to every sequence.

## Example: competing in hearts

After `1♥ - (Pass) - 2♥ - (2♠)`, opener distinguishes:

| Rebid               | Meaning                                           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 2NT, followed by 3♥ | Wants to compete in hearts without showing extras |
| 3♥ directly         | Extra values                                      |
{.styled}

## When it is off

The BridgeHands treatment excludes auctions where:

* RHO passes, doubles, bids at the one level, or makes a cuebid or conventional notrump call.
* RHO makes a natural two-level overcall showing opening values, rather than the competitive action required by the agreement.
* The opponents begin with a strong artificial club.
* Partner opened 1NT: use the agreed defence, including [](#Lebensohl) where applicable.

## Partnership points to confirm

After `1♦ - (Pass) - 1♥ - (2♠)`, some pairs use Good-Bad 2NT; others retain natural 2NT with a stopper. **Agree this explicitly.**

Also settle when partner may depart from 3♣, how to show strong balanced hands, and what happens if opponents bid over 2NT. The weak route can lose the chance to show its suit if the opponents raise again.
