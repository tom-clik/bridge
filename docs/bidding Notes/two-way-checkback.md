**2♣ = invite or stop in diamonds; 2♦ = game force.**

Two-way checkback separates responder's strength while leaving room to find a major-suit fit after opener rebids 1NT. 

## Responder's choices

The following treatment uses direct 2NT as a club relay. This is an agreement to confirm, not a universal part of checkback.

| Rebid over 1NT                            | Meaning                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2♣                                        | Artificial; opener must bid 2♦. Responder passes or makes a natural invitation |
| 2♦                                        | Artificial game force; need not hold diamonds, even if opener bid them         |
| Repeat responder's major at the two level | Weak, normally 5+ cards; not invitational                                      |
| 2♥ after responding 1♠                    | Weak, 5+ spades and 4+ hearts; opener passes or gives preference               |
| 2NT                                       | Artificial; forces 3♣, which responder can pass                                |
{.styled}
| 3x | Weak, 6 card suit (raise or shift)

A direct 2♣ is therefore unavailable as a natural sign-off. A balanced invitation goes through `2♣ - 2♦ - 2NT` in this treatment.

## After the 2♣ relay

Opener's forced 2♦ says nothing about diamonds, strength or major-suit support. Responder now describes the hand. After `1♣ - 1♠ - 1NT - 2♣ - 2♦`:

| Responder's continuation | Meaning                                            |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Pass                     | To play in diamonds                                |
| 2♥                       | Invitational, 5+ spades and 4 hearts               |
| 2♠                       | Invitational, 5+ spades                            |
| 2NT                      | Natural invitation                                 |
| 3♣ / 3♦                  | Natural invitation with the minor                  |
| 3♥                       | Invitational, at least 5–5 in the majors           |
| 3♠                       | Invitational, a good six-card or longer spade suit |
{.styled}

For example, an invitation with five spades:

<bridge style="0_2" info="0">
[North "Opener"]
[South "Responder"]
[Auction "N"]
1C Pass 1S Pass
1NT Pass 2C =1= Pass
2D =2= Pass 2S =3=
[Note "1:Artificial relay."]
[Note "2:Forced; does not show diamonds."]
[Note "3:Invitational with at least five spades; opener may pass."]
</bridge>

Contrast a direct 2♠ over 1NT, which is weak. To stop in diamonds, use the relay and pass 2♦; bidding 2♦ directly would force to game.

## After the game-forcing 2♦

Opener shows in this priority (NB higher priority for 3 card support):

1. 3 card support for partner
2. 4 of other major
3. 2nt or 3nt min, max
